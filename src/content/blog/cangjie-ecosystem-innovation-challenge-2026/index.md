# Cangjie Ecosystem Innovation Challenge: 60 Prizes, Entries Close 7 October

Most coding competitions treat AI assistance as something to police. The Cangjie Ecosystem Innovation Development Challenge scores it instead, up to 10 bonus points, and is unusually specific about what does not count.

![Cangjie Ecosystem Innovation Challenge: 60 Prizes, Entries Close 7 October](./figures/figure-1.jpg)

Third leg: the innovation challenge

The challenge opened on 21 August. It is the third stage of the Cangjie Developer Ecosystem Co-Build Season, following the third-party library drive and the learning stage. The brief is not to solve a set problem. It is to start from a real requirement and ship something that runs and can be verified.

In one sentence: complete a runnable, verifiable innovation project on the Cangjie ecosystem and submit it on AtomGit, the code hosting platform operated by the OpenAtom Foundation.

Two entry routes. Recommended topics cover technical research topics and third-party library development tasks, and more than one team can take the same topic. The organizers publish a running count of how many teams have registered per topic, so you can see where the crowd is before committing. Self-proposed topics are open to anything in the Cangjie ecosystem, but the team runs its own duplication check and attaches a project proposal at submission. Teams that take a community technical topic are also eligible for the annual outstanding technical topic selection.

Third-party libraries, base tooling, core frameworks, data science, AI integration. Students, working engineers and independent developers can all register, solo or in teams of one to four. One submission per team, and picking a topic reserves nothing.

Three steps

1. Register on the AtomGit competition page and pick a topic, or define your own direction.
1. Build. Create the project repository, finish development, testing and documentation, and prepare a demo video and a project overview. 
Third-party library entries must target STS Cangjie 1.1.3, the long-term support release, and be published to the Cangjie central repository following its listing specification. Technical topics follow their own delivery standards.
1. Submit before the deadline for preliminary review. 
The top 30 teams go through to the final, keep iterating, and defend the work in an online roadshow. The preliminary round is scored on quality and completeness; the final on the live demo, the technical approach, what changed between rounds, and the defense itself.

What is on the table

60 prizes, roughly 9,800 USD in total value, awarded as electronics rather than cash.

1. First prize, 1 team, around 1,400 USD
1. Second prize, 2 teams, around 1,100 USD each
1. Third prize, 5 teams, around 560 USD each
1. Merit award, 12 teams, around 140 USD each
1. Participation award, 40 teams, around 42 USD each

Every winning team gets a certificate and community visibility. Strong projects get continued support afterwards, and individual developers who stand out may be offered an AtomGit internship, a collaboration, or a referral to a partner organization.

Where the AI points come from

Using an AI tool to generate code, write tests, fill in documentation or run general optimization earns nothing extra. The points are for giving something back to the ecosystem: a reusable AI tool, Skill, Agent, MCP tool, workflow, prompt template, corpus or example capability built around Cangjie. 

Build it yourself and you submit the repository. Extend an existing project and you submit the PR link plus instructions for running and reusing it. AtomCode and comparable assistants are explicitly encouraged for the everyday work of the project: refactoring, test and doc coverage, performance and security checks, migration, code review, agent development.

Dates, all Beijing time

- 7 October, 23:59:59, preliminary submissions close
- 16 October, finalists announced
- 23 October, 23:59:59, final submissions close
- 24 October, online final judging
- Late October, results
